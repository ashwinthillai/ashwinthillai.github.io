buff 2.0: what's waiting, and who you're meeting

By Ashwin Thillai · November 2026

The first buff was a notebook you typed into, like a terminal. You wrote a line and pressed Send, and buff filed it. It was quick to write in, but you had to remember the right command to get anything back out.

So for 2.0 I asked a narrower question: what is buff for? My answer: buff is the place for short work notes about people and projects. It tells you what's waiting and gets you up to speed before a meeting. Long writing can live in other apps. Every change in 2.0 follows from that one sentence.

Three screens instead of one

buff now opens on Today. It shows what's overdue, what's due and what you're waiting on from other people, with a search box and a quick place to write. People & projects gives everyone you work with a page. Notes is the original terminal, still with exactly two buttons: Send and Options.

A brief without AI

Each page starts with a plain brief: how long since your last note, what they owe you, what you owe them, any open decisions and your last few notes. buff works it out with simple rules, on your phone, in a fraction of a second. If you add a Claude key, a Write it up button turns those notes into a fuller brief, but the plain version is always there.

If you let it read your calendar, Today shows your next meeting with someone you've written about, with a Brief button and an optional reminder ten minutes before. buff only reads your calendar. Nothing from it is saved.

Write like a person

In 1.0 a todo had to start with Do:. Now you write send Maya the slides by Friday and buff shows what it understood before you send: todo · Fri · Maya Chen. Bob will send the spec tomorrow becomes something you're waiting on, and I need coffee stays a note. Every save has an undo.

A real todo list, and widgets

All todos are grouped by when they're due, then by who you're waiting on. Tick a todo to close it, or swipe to snooze it to tomorrow or next week. Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets keep today's list in view, and an optional morning notification tells you how many are due.

Change your mind safely

Touch and hold any note, from any day, to edit or delete it. buff keeps the old text in a history file next to your notes, so every change can be undone, even on another device. The notes themselves are still plain markdown files in your iCloud Drive.

AI that suggests, never decides

On iPhones and iPads with Apple Intelligence, AI is now on by default, because Apple's on-device model is free and private. After you save a note it may offer up to three suggestions, such as a name to tag or a todo it noticed. Nothing changes until you tap one. Turn AI off and everything above still works.

What didn't change

There's still no account, no analytics and no ads, and I can't see your notes. Your notes are files you own and can open on a Mac or in any markdown app.

How 2.0 was built

I wrote the plan and Claude, Anthropic's AI model, wrote the code. For 2.0 several Claude sessions worked in parallel, each with one job: one wrote tests from the spec, another wrote the code to pass them, and a third merged the work. The app now has more than 600 automated tests (how I validate it). I test each part on my own iPhone and iPad, and the screenshots for this post came from a made-up demo notebook.

Try it

buff is free for iPhone and iPad. The buff docs cover every feature, and the buff site has the App Store link once 2.0 is out.
